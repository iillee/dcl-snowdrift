## World layout

- Trees still sit at **64 / 96 / 144 / 88 / 168 / 104 m**. The gaps between them vary with the seed. They are not six even spokes.
- A tree is refused when its trunk would sit on a cliff. Branches may cross the mesh. The trunk keep-out is about **3 m** at the largest scale.
- Snow is cut out on the measured cliff footprint. The ground slab still runs under the cliffs.
- **Six** hidden fires. The first three grow off the hearth. Each later three grow off the three before them, never back off the hearth. A step is **48–80 m**. Nothing sits closer than **48 m** to the hearth or to another pit. A pit is refused on a cliff cell or a tree trunk.
- Buried wood is four bands, all from the hearth. **8** live pieces at **11–18 m**, placed at random, not on a ring and not on the first snow past the 8 m hearth melt. **12** at **15–35 m**. **28** at **35–80 m**, densest at **50 m**. **12** more at **80–160 m**, densest at **110 m** and thinner at the far trees. About **12%** are logs. A piece is refused when its body would cross a cliff cell. Nothing grows back until the world regenerates.

## Fire

**Shipped.** Fuel is one number. It picks a tier, and the tier sets the flame size, the melt ring, the light, and the warmth rate together. What you see is what the fire can do. The five lit tiers are Ember, Low, Warm, Bright, and Roaring. Exact rates live in `src/shared/hearthFuel.ts`.

- The smallest flame still makes light, melt, and some warmth. It is not a dead fire.
- Ember holds the heat bar through dawn and day. From dusk it loses to the cold, so it slows a freeze and does not prevent one.
- Low and every tier above it beat the night cold and restore warmth.
- A torch can put the spark back. That spark is Ember, not a full tank. Relighting does not mint a safe fire. Wood does.
- For the 2026-10-01 test the crowd drain is `1 + 0.5 * log2(players)`. One player is 1×. Twenty are about 3.2×, and night still doubles that. Wood counts, tree yield, branch and log seconds, the fuel cap, and the day/night multipliers are unchanged.

Flame is whether ignition continues. Fuel is the wood stored in the fire. Heat is what the current tier can do. Survival is whether that heat beats the cold.

> Torch preserves flame. Weak fire buys time. Wood creates safety.

Standing next to another player does not warm you. Heat comes from your lit torch or a campfire.

## Freeze and the end of the run

**Shipped, first pass.** A full frost bar freezes you in the ice where you stand. You stay in the world. Your torch goes dark and keeps its fuel. Carried wood stays with you. Another player's lit torch, held on the ice, thaws you where you fell. The seconds on that thaw are still playtest tuning.

- If a fire is still lit, frozen players wake at that fire. Waking there leaves **three** gold segments, so a night ember does not refreeze you in the stand-up.
- If no fire is lit, frozen players stay frozen. They do not simply respawn.
- The run ends when every player still connected is frozen and no fire is left to wake them. A player who is still moving can relight a dark fire and recover the group. Losing the last flame is a crisis, not by itself the end.
- When the run does end, carried wood is wiped and the torch is emptied. There is no wood pile.

The cards read: **After 1 day, the world's flame goes out.** (or **After 2 days,** and so on). **Centuries pass in the cold.** Then one card, two lines: **A new hearth is kindled.** / **Don't let the fire die.** That card is also the cold open. It stays up while the new world fades in. Dawn still starts when the screen first hits black. Cards 1 and 2 hold for 2.5 s again.

Older copy is retired: nights survived, "Centuries pass in the dark," "A new civilization begins," and "the last fire going out ends the world at that instant."

## Seeds

- A new server start draws a fresh seed. It is not the UTC day number, so leaving and coming back does not rebuild the first layout of the day.
- Every regen draws another fresh seed.
- If the server is still running when you rejoin, you return to the world already in progress.

## Phone and the ? menu

- The native **+** that opened extra buttons is gone. Mute sits in that slot. Eye, help, and mute stay on the cluster. Jump and interact stay.
- Overhead view on a phone shows **+** and **−** in a row under the pan pad, above eye, help, and jump. Desktop zoom stays beside the eye.
- The ? menu, on desktop and on a phone, is three lines: **Day N**, then **Dawn: 0:12** (the live time of day and the countdown), then **Don't let the fire die**. The explore line, the wood line, the fires-lit count, and the version chip are gone.
